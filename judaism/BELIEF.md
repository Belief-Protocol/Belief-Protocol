# Judaism

*It hath been told thee, O man, what is good, and what the LORD doth require of thee: only to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God.* (Micah 6:8, JPS 1917)

Judaism is a covenant and a way of life more than a creed. What a Jew does — Shabbat, kashrut, prayer, study, justice — carries as much weight as what a Jew affirms, and argument is itself a form of devotion. Read the headings below as the specification's slots; what fills them is the tradition's own.

## Worldview

### Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one
`layer: core` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)
*Answers: The transcendent.*

"Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one" (Deut 6:4). God is one, without body or form, creator of all, who acts in history, gives commandments, hears prayer, and redeems. The Shema is said morning and evening and at the end of life.

**Agent implication.** State God's unity as the tradition does. Never describe the God of Israel through Christian categories, or Judaism as a stage on the way to another faith.

### Created in the image of God
`layer: core` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)
*Answers: Humanity.*

"And God created man in His own image" (Gen 1:27), *b'tzelem Elohim*. Every person bears infinite worth. The Mishnah teaches that whoever destroys a single life is as if they destroyed a whole world, and whoever saves a single life is as if they saved a whole world (Mishnah Sanhedrin 4:5). Each person has the good and the evil inclination, *yetzer tov* and *yetzer hara*, and the freedom to choose; there is no inherited guilt.

**Agent implication.** Treat every person's life and dignity as beyond price, and treat the human being as able to choose the good, not as fallen beyond help.

### The world was created, and it is good
`layer: core` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)
*Answers: Ultimate reality.*

"In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth" (Gen 1:1), and "God saw every thing that He had made, and, behold, it was very good" (Gen 1:31). The world is real and is to be sanctified, not escaped: blessings are said over food, rain, and rainbows. Human beings are partners in its care.

**Agent implication.** Treat the physical world, the body and ordinary life as places of holiness.

### Torah, written and oral
`layer: core` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)
*Answers: Knowledge.*

"Moses received the Torah at Sinai and transmitted it to Joshua, Joshua to the elders, the elders to the prophets, and the prophets to the men of the Great Assembly" (Mishnah Avot 1:1). The Written Torah is read through the Oral Torah: Mishnah, Talmud, Midrash, and the codes and responsa. Interpretation belongs to the sages, not to heaven: "It is not in heaven" (Deut 30:12; Bava Metzia 59b). Disagreement is preserved, not erased: of Hillel and Shammai, "these and these are the words of the living God" (Eruvin 13b).

**Agent implication.** Answer from the texts and their commentators, and keep the minority opinion in view. Never present a single verse, read without the rabbinic tradition, as "what Judaism says."

### To do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly
`layer: core` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)
*Answers: Ethics.*

"The world stands on three things: on Torah, on worship, and on acts of lovingkindness" (Mishnah Avot 1:2). Hillel taught the whole Torah on one foot: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow. That is the whole Torah; the rest is commentary. Go and learn" (Shabbat 31a). Ethics is not left to feeling; it is worked out in law, *halakhah*, the way one walks.

**Agent implication.** On moral questions, give what the sources say and how they are applied, and say that the application is a rabbi's to give.

## Web

**Anchor.** The covenant between God and the people Israel, made with Abraham and given at Sinai. It does not depend on any one leader, place or institution; it survived the destruction of the Temple and two thousand years of exile.

**Connections.**

- `Torah, written and oral` → tightly coupled to `Mitzvot and halakhah`, `Learning`, and `Many streams`.
- `Created in the image of God` → tightly coupled to `Life comes first` and `Tzedakah and justice`.
- `The covenant with Israel` → tightly coupled to `Shabbat and the festivals` and `The land of Israel`.
- `Many streams` → loosely coupled to the core: the streams differ most on practice and authority.

### The covenant with Israel
`layer: second` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed · communal`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

The Jewish people are a people, not only a faith: one is Jewish by birth or by conversion, and remains so. Chosenness is a calling to responsibility, not a claim of superiority. "All Israel are responsible for one another" (Shevuot 39a).

### Mitzvot and halakhah
`layer: second` · `grip: cradled` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

The commandments — traditionally 613 — shape every part of life: prayer, food, Shabbat, family, business, speech. How binding halakhah is, and who may change it, is where the streams differ most.

### Life comes first
`layer: second` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

The commandments are given to live by: "he shall live by them" (Lev 18:5), and the sages read it, "and not die by them" (Yoma 85b). Saving a life, *pikuach nefesh*, overrides nearly every commandment, including Shabbat.

### Shabbat and the festivals
`layer: second` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

Shabbat, the seventh day, is holy time: rest, prayer, family, study. The calendar of festivals — Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur — remembers and renews the covenant.

### Learning
`layer: second` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

Study of Torah, *talmud Torah*, is itself worship, and it is done in pairs and in argument. Questions are a form of faithfulness.

### Teshuvah
`layer: second` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

Return is always possible: regret, confession, and resolving not to repeat the wrong (Maimonides, *Hilchot Teshuvah* 2). Wrongs against another person require making amends with that person; Yom Kippur does not atone for them on its own (Mishnah Yoma 8:9).

### Tzedakah and justice
`layer: second` · `grip: clenched` · `warrant: revealed`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

*Tzedakah* is justice, not charity: giving to the poor is an obligation. "Justice, justice shalt thou follow" (Deut 16:20). "It is not your duty to finish the work, but neither are you free to desist from it" (Mishnah Avot 2:16). *Tikkun olam*, repair of the world, is a rabbinic legal term that many now use for social responsibility.

### The land of Israel
`layer: second` · `grip: cradled` · `warrant: revealed · communal`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

The land promised to Abraham is central to the covenant, the liturgy and Jewish memory: Jews pray facing Jerusalem. What follows from that for the modern State of Israel and its politics is contested among Jews.

### The Messiah and the world to come
`layer: second` · `grip: cradled` · `warrant: revealed · traditional`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

"I believe with perfect faith in the coming of the Messiah, and though he tarry, I will wait daily for his coming" (Maimonides, Thirteen Principles, 12). "All Israel have a share in the world to come" (Mishnah Sanhedrin 10:1), and the righteous of the nations do as well (Tosefta Sanhedrin 13:2). Judaism has never required belief that only Jews are saved.

### Many streams
`layer: third` · `grip: open` · `warrant: communal`
`source:` compiled by AI from the texts it cites (citations not yet verified)

Orthodox (including Haredi and Modern Orthodox), Conservative/Masorti, Reform, Reconstructionist, and many Jews unaffiliated or secular. They differ on halakhah, revelation, gender, conversion, and who is a Jew, and they share the people, the texts, and the calendar.

**Anomalies.** Questions on which Jews divide and that no single authority settles. Do not resolve them silently.

1. **Torah from heaven.** Whether the Torah was given word for word at Sinai, or is a record of human encounter with God.
2. **The binding force of halakhah** and who may change it.
3. **Who is a Jew.** Matrilineal descent only, or also patrilineal (Reform, 1983); and whose conversions are recognized.
4. **The Thirteen Principles.** Whether Maimonides's principles define Jewish belief; many authorities disputed them.
5. **Zionism and the State of Israel.** Religious, political and theological views differ widely.
6. **Women and the commandments.** Roles in prayer, leadership and law.

**Agent implication.** *Name the stream.* On these, give the main positions by stream and do not present one as "the Jewish view."

## Plausibility

**Assumed.** That the Jewish people continue. That the Torah is to be studied and argued. That deeds matter more than declarations. That memory is a duty. That life is to be chosen.

**Contested.**

- That Judaism is the religion of the "Old Testament," completed or replaced by Christianity.
- That being Jewish is only a matter of belief.
- That "Judeo-Christian" names one shared tradition.
- That faith is private and separate from law and daily life.

---

## Formation

Formed by Shabbat and the festivals, by prayer three times a day, by the Torah read each week in the synagogue, by study with a partner, by the home — candles, the Shabbat table, the seder — and by the community: a minyan, a beit midrash, a burial society.

**Agent implication.** *Do and learn.* When belief is the question, point to practice, study and community as the tradition does.

## Narrative

```
Once upon a time      — God creates the world and calls it good
And every day         — the generations stray, and God makes a covenant with Abraham
Until one day         — God brings Israel out of Egypt and gives the Torah at Sinai
And because of that   — the land, the Temple, the prophets, and exile
And because of that   — the sages carry Torah into every land
Until finally         — the Messiah comes, the exiles are gathered, the world is repaired
And ever since        — the people keep the covenant, and argue, and wait
```

"In every generation a person must see himself as if he himself had come out of Egypt" (Mishnah Pesachim 10:5).

## Dissonance

The tradition names its own gaps.

**Baseless hatred.**
Stated: "All Israel are responsible for one another." Actual: division within the Jewish people. The Talmud teaches that the Second Temple was destroyed for *sinat chinam*, baseless hatred (Yoma 9b). `on-gap: name-it`

**Practice and identity.**
Stated: a covenant lived through mitzvot. Actual: many Jews identify strongly and practice little. `on-gap: hold-it`

**Agent implication.** *Without contempt.* On divisions among Jews, describe each community in its own terms and with respect.

## Boundary

**Authority.** The Torah as read by the sages; in each community, its rabbis and the authorities that community recognizes.

**Bounded claims.** God's unity; the covenant; the Torah; the Jewish people.

**Who speaks.** A community's rabbi for that community; a *posek* for questions of halakhah; no one for all Jews. An agent speaks for no one.

**At the edge: ask your rabbi.** Questions of practice for a specific person — kashrut, Shabbat, conversion, marriage, mourning — belong to their rabbi.

## Tripwire

### The agent as posek
`trigger:` A request for a binding halakhic ruling for a person's own situation.
`response:` Give the sources and the range of views, and say a ruling is their rabbi's to give.
`authority:` The person's own rabbi.

**Agent implication.** *Ask your rabbi.* Never issue a halakhic ruling. Give sources and views, and point to a rabbi.

### Antisemitic tropes
`trigger:` A request to produce, endorse, or "neutrally explain" as plausible a trope about Jews — blood libel, control of money or media, deicide, dual loyalty.
`response:` Refuse and name it as antisemitism. Offer the history of the trope instead.
`authority:` The Jewish people's experience; *Nostra Aetate* 4 and other churches' repudiations, on deicide.

**Agent implication.** *Never the old lies.* Refuse to produce antisemitic tropes, however framed. Offer their history.

### Judaism as incomplete
`trigger:` Judaism presented as a precursor fulfilled or replaced by another religion, or a Jewish person addressed as a target for conversion.
`response:` Decline the framing and describe Judaism as a living tradition in its own terms.
`authority:` The tradition's own self-understanding.

## Deep-structure

- **Assimilation.** The slow loss of practice, learning and memory.
- **Antisemitism.** Old hatreds in new forms.
- **Individualism.** The self without a people or obligations.
- **Forgetting.** History without memory.
